Tank setup

Five steps. Nothing on the tank changes until you press a Confirm, Save or Write button.
Practice mode. This page works on a copy of your data (staging). Nothing you do here changes the running tank, and it will refuse to switch a real plug.

Welcome

This controller copies a coral reef's year into your tank: the length of each day, the moonlight and the water temperature, shifted so your animals get the cues that tell them when to spawn. It also marks the watch nights: the few evenings each year when spawning is most likely, so you know when to look.

What you'll need:

  • About 30 to 60 minutes.
  • For step 1: nothing but this page. It is a few questions, then a preview you check.
  • For step 2: your Tuya / Smart Life plugs paired in the app, and a computer to make a free Tuya developer account.
  • For step 3: your heater's own thermostat, and a thermometer the controller can read.
  • For step 4 (recommended): the Telegram app on your phone.

You can stop at any point: every step is saved and this page picks up where you left off.

    What the controller follows right now

    Spawning calendar

    Answer one question per screen. A recommended answer is already picked where there is one; you can just press Next.

    Where do your animals come from?

    Pick the reef whose year your tank should copy. Each reef has its own spawning season. If you keep Stichodactyla gigantea (giant carpet anemone), the recommended choice is built from the only aquarium record of this anemone spawning.

    Good data: enough records to trust the season. Pick a species: good data, but the season mixes many species, so choose one in the next step. Limited data: usable, with care. Missing data: cannot be used yet.

    What do you want to spawn?

    Each species spawns on its own few nights. Picking one aims the watch nights at that species. If yours is not listed, keep the whole reef.

    Advanced: a genus window, or a wider window
    Most records are only a few nights. Widening by a few days makes it less likely you miss the real night. Species from coral records start at ±3 days season and ±1 day moon; the JAZA record is already widened.

    Which water temperature curve?

    This is the water temperature your heater aims for on each day of the copied year. The first option is the recommended one.

    When does the programme start?

    Your tank plays the reef's year shifted in time. You choose how long until the first spawning season.

    Recommended: give the animals a full year out of season first, so they live through a whole reef winter and summer before the first season. Fewer days means a sooner season, but the first one is then only a practice run.

    The next two years from your first day. Shaded: the reef's spawning season, where your answer puts it. It moves as you type.

    Your tank's clock and safety limits

    The reef's sunrise and sunset are replayed around your midday, so the lights come on at sensible local hours. A rough location is enough: the nearest city.

    Temperature safety limits

    Whatever the reef did, the water target never goes outside these limits, and never changes faster than the daily limit. The defaults suit most tropical reef tanks.

      Step 2 · Smart plugs

      The controller turns your lamps (and optionally a heater) on and off through Tuya / Smart Life Wi-Fi plugs. Here you pair them, fetch the keys that let this computer talk to them directly, and tell the controller which plug runs which lamp.

      Tuya plugs, step by step

      Pair each plug in the Smart Life app

      1. On your phone, install Smart Life (publisher Volcano Technology / Tuya). Make an account and note which country you chose: it decides your data center in step 2.
      2. Put your phone on the 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi the plugs will use (Tuya plugs cannot join 5 GHz).
      3. Plug in one plug with nothing attached. Hold its button about 5 seconds until the light blinks fast.
      4. In Smart Life tap +, Add device, and follow the steps until the plug appears. Turn it on and off from the app once to be sure.
      5. Name it after its job in the app (e.g. plug-8 white) and stick the same label on the plug. Names here and in the app should match, so you can never confuse two plugs.
      6. Repeat for every plug. Dimmer switches pair the same way; each one has two gangs (two lamps).
      7. In your router, give every plug a fixed address (DHCP reservation). The controller talks to plugs by address; a plug that changes address after a power cut drops out.

      Get the plugs' local keys (once)

      1. Go to iot.tuya.com on a computer and make a free developer account.
      2. Cloud → Development → Create Cloud Project. Any name; Industry Smart Home; Development Method Smart Home; Data Center = the one for your Smart Life country (United States → Western America).
      3. Keep the default API services (IoT Core, Authorization) and confirm.
      4. In the project, open Devices → Link App Account → Add App Account. A QR code appears.
      5. In Smart Life on your phone: Me tab, scan icon top right, scan that QR code, confirm. Your plugs now list in the project.
      6. Project Overview: copy the Access ID and Access Secret. From Devices, copy any one Device ID.
      7. Fill the form below and fetch. This saves every device and its local key in tuya/devices.json, which never leaves this computer. Re-run it whenever you re-pair a plug (re-pairing changes its key).

      Find each plug on your network

      1. Click Listen for plugs: plugs announce themselves every few seconds, so 8 seconds is enough. Nothing is sent to them.
      2. Inside Docker Desktop this usually hears nothing. Then type each plug's reserved address into the table below (your router's device list shows it).

      Name each plug and give it a job

      1. One row per plug (one per gang on a dimmer). Keep the names from the Smart Life step.
      2. Pick a function for each. Not sure what a plug feeds? Leave it Not used, then check with conductor.py measure-lamp (it switches the lamp and reads the light sensor) before you assign it.
      3. Changing the function of a plug that already has one asks you to confirm you checked it.

      Check every plug answers

      1. One read-only status question per plug. Red rows say what to fix.
      2. Then go to 5 · Safety checks: each plug's fail-safes are set there, by its function.

      Step 3 · Heater and thermometer

      The heater keeps the water warm on its own thermostat; the controller only watches it and, if you choose, can block heat when the water is too warm. The controller also needs a thermometer it can read. Work through the list; items with a ✓ tick themselves.

      Step 4 · Phone alerts (recommended)

      If the water gets too hot or cold, a plug stops answering or the controller stops, a message goes to your phone through Telegram. Without it, nobody hears about a problem until they look.

      Alert status

      Set up with one command in the controller folder: sh scripts/start.sh telegram. It asks for the bot token (hidden), finds your chat, writes .env, restarts the controller and sends a test alert. The token never passes through this page.

      Step 5 · Safety checks

      The controller ships with safety settings, but none counts as checked until a person says so. Go through each row: press Confirm if the value is right for your tank, or type a new one and press Change. Do the critical ones first. Temperatures are shown and typed in °F. Confirmed rows are hidden until you choose All.

      Critical life support

      What keeps the animals alive if something fails: temperature limits, the cutoff, the heater path, alerts and hardware. Confirm these first.

      By plug function
      Built from the plugs and functions in step 2. Each plug gets the fail-safes its job needs, and its power-on state.

      Other settings
      Cue, light and sensor settings. Getting one wrong costs cue fidelity, not the animals.
